THE ENGINEER BEHIND THE BOARDS

From embedded circuits to digital hardware.

I’m an embedded hardware and electronics engineer with a strong interest in how computing is built below the software layer: power delivery, signal paths, interfaces, timing, and the physical constraints that shape a reliable system.

My work spans multilayer PCB design, mixed-signal measurement, RF-aware layouts, power architecture and FPGA-based acquisition platforms. I like understanding why a design works, documenting the decisions, and making the next engineering step explicit.

My longer-term direction is toward digital hardware architecture, IC/ASIC design and semiconductor engineering, while continuing to build practical board-level experience.

HOW I APPROACH A DESIGN

Good engineering is connected thinking.

Hardware decisions rarely live in isolation. The board, firmware, interfaces, power budget and test strategy have to agree.

01

Start with constraints

Define interfaces, power rails, timing, mechanical limits and manufacturing needs before committing to the layout.

02

Design for the return path

Stack-up, reference planes, current loops and connector transitions are part of the circuit—not finishing touches.

03

Make decisions reviewable

Keep schematics, requirements, calculations, BOMs and manufacturing outputs clear enough for another engineer to verify.

04

Validate what is claimed

Separate simulation from measurement, a design from a built board, and a working prototype from a qualified product.
